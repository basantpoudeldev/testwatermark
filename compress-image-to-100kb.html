Compress an image to 100KB

Set an exact target file size and Proofmark reduces quality — and resolution if needed — to hit it, without you having to guess a quality percentage.

Try it now — free, and nothing you upload leaves your browser.

Compress an image now

Many forms, portals, and upload fields cap file size at 100KB, but most photos straight off a phone or camera are several megabytes. Instead of guessing a JPEG quality setting and re-exporting repeatedly, Proofmark's "target size" compression mode does the guessing for you: pick 100KB (or 50KB, 200KB, or any custom size), and it automatically reduces quality first, then resolution if quality reduction alone can't reach the target, until your file lands at or under that size.

Why file size targets are better than quality sliders

A "quality: 80%" setting means something different for every photo — a simple photo with lots of flat color compresses much smaller than a highly detailed one at the same quality setting. Targeting an exact KB size guarantees the output fits whatever limit you're working against, every time, regardless of what's in the photo.

Frequently asked questions

Does compressing to 100KB reduce image dimensions?

Only if needed. Proofmark first tries reducing quality alone at full resolution. If a photo is large or detailed enough that quality reduction can't reach 100KB on its own, it then reduces resolution too, always trying to preserve as much quality as possible while still hitting your target.

Can I compress multiple photos at once?

Yes — batch upload is supported. Every photo in the batch gets compressed to the same target size and bundled into a single ZIP download.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. Compression happens entirely in your browser using the Canvas API — your file is never sent to a server.
